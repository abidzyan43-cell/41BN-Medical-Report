Gunshot Wound — First Aid Guide

Emergency: Call local emergency services immediately and arrange urgent hospital transfer. Do not delay transport to complete this guide. Use gloves/PPE if available.

Illustrated First-Aid Poster

Original poster supplied in this chat. Displayed in full, with contain sizing so no part is cropped.

First aid by injury location (head to toe)

All suspected gunshot wounds are life-threatening even if the entry wound looks small. Do not probe the wound, remove bullets/embedded objects, give food or drink, or attempt definitive treatment in the field.

🧠 Head / Face

Call emergency help; keep the person still and monitor breathing and consciousness. Apply gentle pressure only to external bleeding sites where safe. Do not press on an exposed skull injury, eye, or embedded object. Do not block the mouth or nose.

🫁 Neck

Urgent emergency transfer. Apply gentle pressure around an external bleeding point without compressing the airway or both sides of the neck. Do not wrap a bandage tightly around the neck; do not remove embedded objects.

🫀 Chest

Call emergency help. For an open chest wound, use a purpose-made vented chest seal if trained and available; otherwise follow dispatcher/approved trauma protocol. Do not seal with an improvised credit card or airtight material. Watch for worsening breathing.

🩻 Abdomen / Back / Pelvis

Do not push organs or objects back in. Cover exposed tissue loosely with a sterile dressing moistened with saline if available. Do not apply pressure over protruding organs. Keep warm and arrange urgent transfer.

💪 Arms / Hands

Control severe bleeding with firm direct pressure and a dressing. For life-threatening limb bleeding, use a commercial tourniquet above the wound (not over a joint) if trained; tighten until bleeding stops and note the time. Do not loosen it.

🦵 Legs / Feet

Control severe bleeding with direct pressure; use a commercial tourniquet for life-threatening limb bleeding if trained. Do not elevate a suspected fracture or painful injured limb. Keep the person still and warm.

🩸 Shock / Unresponsive

Lay the person flat if tolerated and there is no breathing difficulty or other reason not to; keep warm. Do not give food or drink. If unresponsive and not breathing normally, begin CPR and use an AED if available, following training/dispatcher instructions.

🚑 Before transfer

Recheck breathing and bleeding continuously. Do not remove bullets or embedded objects. Record the injury time, tourniquet time if used, and any change in consciousness/breathing; hand over to the receiving medical team.

Training reference only. Follow current ITBP/TACTICAL COMBAT CASUALTY CARE protocols, certified training, and receiving clinician instructions.
